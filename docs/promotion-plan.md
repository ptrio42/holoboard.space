# Holoboard promotion plan, October 2026 to January 2027

Working plan for 19 October 2026 through 10 January 2027. Small Nostr/Bitcoin
projects and people who enjoy discovering them are the initial audience
hypothesis. Use this segment as a starting point while allowing other relevant
notes in the campaign. The weekly time limit is one hour, including selection,
publishing and conversation. Dates are editorial windows, not scheduled
publications.

## Direction

Frame Holoboard as a board for notes people want others to notice, their own or
someone else's. Give readers concrete reasons to look and return. Start from
ptrio42's framing: smaller communities can have places of their own while
remaining open to the wider internet. Nostr is the underlying technology;
Holoboard's purpose is discovering and bringing attention to content people care
about. General introductions should make sense without knowing Nostr. The
initial distribution channel and audience hypothesis do not define the product
as Nostr-only. Sats express "I'd like you to see this."
They prove neither quality nor a human author.

Holoboard currently supplies one paid board. Project highlights are one possible
campaign topic alongside useful writing, event announcements and requests for
participation. Select examples for their relevance to the intended readers;
there is no required category mix. Campaign selection does not narrow the
product's admission rules or turn its ranking into an editorial selection.
Being a place worth checking is an ambition to earn through useful material and
participation. Recurring readership is not established yet.

The reader should get a concrete reason to open the selected note. A promoter
should understand how to give an existing note visibility, including someone
else's. Connect these experiences by showing real notes and following what
happens around them. Feature explanations support a specific reading or
promotion task.

## Campaign shape and accounts

Use one main Nostr post per week. Most posts highlight one relevant note, with a
short reason to look and a direct destination. A monthly builder post explains a
choice or reports an actual observation. It replaces that week's product post.
The published 15 October release announcement remains the launch reference.

Holoboard speaks for the board: discoveries, useful routes and clear payment
explanations. ptrio42 speaks personally about the purpose and experience of
building it. Quote between accounts when the second voice adds something; a
duplicate repost is not another required campaign task.

Use English for the existing account audience. Keep the casual builder voice
without a compulsory punchline. Prefer an ordinary note or native quote over
custom media. Existing product screenshots can support a relevant explanation;
new visual production does not fit the base weekly budget.

The base plan uses organic Nostr distribution and has no required cash spend.
It does not require subsidizing placements to fill the board. Any later paid
campaign needs a separate budget. Planning does not authorize publications,
outreach or payments; prepare the specific content before requesting execution.

This is a queue of useful jobs, not a demand for filler. A relevant question or
brief factual update can replace a missing story. Leave a slot unused
when there is nothing worthwhile to publish. After eight weeks, expand only if
there is evidence of reader value. Try one willing contributor within the same
hour by replacing a selection task, rather than starting another channel.

## Weekly routine, 60 minutes

| Minutes | Work |
| --- | --- |
| 15 | Select one note and check its source, current board membership and destination. |
| 20 | Adapt the short post, check the actual client preview and complete approved publication. |
| 20 | Take part in relevant conversation, including one reader follow-up when useful. |
| 5 | Record observations and the next editorial choice. |

Use the same day each week for convenience, with no claim that a particular
weekday or hour performs best on Nostr. At the month-end review, use the
conversation allocation to assess results. Do not add a separate meeting.
If selection or discussion needs more time, simplify the post. Budget figures
are planning allocations, not measured production times.

For distribution, connect the post to an existing relevant conversation when
there is something substantive to add. Credit the original author. Keep the
call to action on one useful destination. Do not require people to pay before
they can participate in a conversation or read the board.

## Selection and presentation

Select a note with a concrete reason for the intended readers to investigate,
participate or share. Start with the chosen Nostr/Bitcoin audience and assess
other subjects for relevance as candidates arise. Check the original note and
primary sources for its claims. Use one note per post by default. Do not infer
that the author paid, that the author endorses Holoboard or that a discovery
was caused by the campaign.

Campaign editorial choices are separate from paid board position. A selected
note can later leave the active board. Its discussion can remain a useful
destination, but refresh any wording that says it is currently promoted. An
off-board suggestion can be discussed with its status explicit; it does not
need an undisclosed founder-funded promotion to qualify for conversation.

Check the exact copy, links and quote together in a phone client preview before
publication. Preserve readable spacing and one clear next action. Word count
alone does not establish density. The personal builder quote was checked with
its actual quoted announcement in Primal and Coracle, using Chrome and WebKit
phone browser viewports with intercepted reads and an ephemeral personal
identity. The three subsequent campaign drafts still need their own checks.

## Measurement and decisions

The main question is whether readers find something useful and choose to come
back. Track small amounts of direct evidence before building more analytics.

| Evidence | Collection within the routine | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A reader names a useful find | Brief voluntary conversation; note what they found and whether they already knew it. | Reported discovery or renewed interest. |
| An earlier reader reports another visit | One later follow-up; ask what prompted the visit and distinguish it from the current reminder. | Reported return behavior, not measured site retention. |
| Someone reports promoting a note | Ask whether it was their own or another person's and whether help or a subsidy was involved. | Reported participation; keep founder-supported cases separate. |
| Substantive replies to campaign posts | Record the question, contribution or repeated misunderstanding. | Topics and friction worth addressing. |
| Public promotion data | Inspect first-paid dates and totals when relevant. | Activity on the board, without identifying anonymous payers or attributing it to a post. |

Count evidence once per participating account across the pilot and distinguish
accounts from verified people. Exclude ptrio42 and Holoboard from external
participant counts. Use aggregate notes or public references in the local
review file; do not copy private correspondence into promotional claims.
Unobserved values stay blank, not zero. A reader who does not answer is not a
confirmed non-returner.

As a working decision aid, aim by week eight to hear from three external
respondents who can name a useful find, including two who report a later visit.
Also look for an external promotion made without a founder subsidy. These
small counts are proposed learning targets, not forecasts, benchmarks,
statistical validation or a mandatory all-or-nothing pass condition.

After four weeks, improve the weakest part of the path: relevance of selected notes,
clarity of the post, destination or distribution. After eight weeks, continue
the same rhythm if reader value repeats. If there are replies but no useful
finds, change the selection. If there is little exposure, try a more relevant
conversation before rejecting the idea. If readers return but nobody promotes,
investigate whether they understand the paid contribution and see a reason to
use it. At week twelve, keep the part that works and change one weak assumption.

### Available data and limits

The public API reported 43 active promoted notes on 18 October, with 21 entries
on the first board page. This is a dated inventory baseline, not a count of
users, readers or promoters. The API exposes campaign data rather than visits;
see [the campaign API](../relay/API.md#read-board-and-waiting-room-campaigns).
Exact signed candidates and observation time are in the local `baseline.json`.

No reader-return analytics or private analytics dashboard was verified for this
plan. Previously observed Cloudflare telemetry does not establish accessible
campaign data. Cloudflare documents [visits and page views](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/data-metrics/high-level-metrics/)
as traffic metrics; they are not a count of the same people returning. Treat any
available traffic trends as supporting context, not an attribution experiment.
[NIP-25 reactions](https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nips/blob/master/25.md)
are reactions to events, not evidence that someone visited Holoboard. Observed
reaction and reply counts also depend on which relays were checked.

## Introductory page prototype

A possible `/about` page is prepared as an isolated local
[prototype](../output/promotion/landing-v1/README.md). It introduces the board,
free reading, paid visibility and promoting someone else's note. Frozen examples
include a recommendation, a tutorial and a photograph's caption. The application
has not been changed or deployed for this prototype.

A [device imagery variant](../output/promotion/landing-v1/device-study/README.md)
uses generated laptop and phone illustrations based on actual browser captures.
The current graphics show the updated navigation from local application build
`46dcd314ba64556e79539e491a034a17dd7045cc`, with the production feature profile
and public read endpoints; that navigation was not yet deployed when captured.
It supplies a separate portrait phone image at narrow widths.
Exact source screenshots remain available to distinguish the illustrations
from the captured interface and from physical-device verification.

The [current illustrated page](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md)
keeps the accepted device hero and living-neighborhood promotion scene. Its
introduction now follows the broader positioning above, with Nostr identified
in a quiet footer link. "A small place, open to the wider world" carries the
builder's motivation. The [mechanism section](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#how-it-works)
uses actual local frontend captures in one persistent Holoboard window. Native
scrolling reveals the board, Add to board editor, invoice and ranked result.
Keep the product's real card layout and navigation recognizable. A simple
abstract visualization is a separate direction; avoid mixing it with lookalike
UI. Upward scrolling reverses the sequence; a skip link and static fallback keep
the explanation available. Fictional examples cover music, an event and writing. Distinguish the top 21 from other active paid posts;
the demo makes no direct text/link creation availability claim.
The broader positioning is accepted; this section's visual treatment is a
local review candidate. Supporting artwork needs an agreed content brief before
further generation. Use readable
metaphors that work with the adjacent copy, with the app's illustrated cyberpunk
world, deep violet, cyan and pink neon as the visual treatment. Vary the subject,
silhouette and composition across sections. Earlier city scenes were not
accepted: repeating neon noticeboards supplied atmosphere without adding enough
meaning. Flat UI fragments, phone-like enclosures and the warm architectural
diorama are also outside the intended direction. Literal payment diagrams and
number sequences belong in explanations rather than defining the artwork.
The [working illustration brief](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#working-content-brief)
separates each message from its proposed visual metaphor. Keep the accepted
projector composition as a comparison beside the promotion copy. Its printed
publication belongs to that metaphor; it does not define the content treatment
for a digital billboard. The remaining metaphors still need review.
These images must not imply
guaranteed readership, content quality or an implemented multi-board feature.

A [billboard alternative](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#billboard-alternative)
explores the product name's billboard inspiration and cyberpunk digital signage.
It changes only the promotion image. "Small place" can describe one board in the
wider Nostr network; the visual scale of a city display does not establish mass
reach. Compare this public-display metaphor with the projector's more personal
gesture of recommendation before choosing the artwork.

The art direction remains exploratory. The user's Blade Runner association adds
layered architecture, material wear and advertising integrated into the city to
the brief, without making the imagery oppressively dark. Cyan and pink neon
remain part of Holoboard's identity. Explore lighting, atmosphere and urban
character separately before generating another candidate. A billboard's Small
Signals content should read as a digital note, with an author and text rendered
by the display, rather than as a scanned illustrated paper cover.

The [two city studies](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#two-cyberpunk-directions)
compare a living neon neighborhood with a cinematic city after rain. They keep
the same fictional digital note and closely matched composition, with identical
landing copy. They separate the feeling of an approachable local place from the
atmospheric depth of mist and luminous reflections.

The [neighborhood motion prototype](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#living-neon-neighborhood-in-motion)
uses the selected living neon neighborhood for a short scroll-controlled scene:
the digital note appears, then its light reaches the surrounding city and wet
pavement. The phone sequence is shorter. Normal scrolling, readable copy and a
complete static fallback remain available. This local visual study does not
change the application or establish campaign results.

The [living-neighborhood extension](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#a-living-neighborhood)
adds depth between the billboard building and the wider city, with subtle vapor,
transit light and distant neon movement. The digital note remains the focal
point. Ambient movement pauses offscreen and can be stopped explicitly. The
living-neighborhood scene is accepted. Keep the earlier motion prototype
available for comparison.

The [Paid visibility study](../output/promotion/landing-v1/section-study/README.md#paid-visibility-energy-module)
uses the selected energy-module motif: a close view of a worn coupler and a
gold pulse weakening along a translucent cable. The copy explains ranking
weight and its decay; the image supplies a temporary-energy metaphor. Review
this rendering beside its copy. The stable local `/about-current/` page combines
it with the accepted neighborhood, while preserving the other sections.

Posts about Holoboard's purpose could link to this introduction after review
and integration. Posts about a particular note should keep their direct
destination. Review the exact phone and desktop page for comprehension before
claiming that it improves campaign results.
