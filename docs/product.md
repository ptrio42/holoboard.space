# Holoboard product context

This document records the product's purpose and working audience assumptions.
The [promotion plan](promotion-plan.md) sets campaign priorities and a time
budget; those choices do not restrict the product's audience or subject matter.

## Purpose

Holoboard is a public board for things people want others to notice. Anyone can
give a post paid visibility, including a post written by someone else. Readers
can browse freely and investigate the posts, links and conversations they find.

The motivating idea is that smaller communities can have places of their own
while remaining open to the wider internet. Holoboard should earn visits through
what people discover and choose to bring to attention. Nostr supplies underlying
technology; using or advocating the protocol is not the product's reason to
exist or a boundary on its intended audience or subject matter.

Holoboard currently provides one board. A broader ecosystem of smaller places
is an aspiration, not an implemented multi-board feature.

## Who it serves

Promoters want to draw attention to something: their project, writing, event or
request for participation, or someone else's contribution they want to recommend.
Promoting another person's post is a core use case. The board's paid ranking
lets them contribute to placement independently of follower counts.

Readers want something worth opening and a reason to return. Useful discoveries
and the conversations around them are part of the intended value. A board needs
to earn recurring readership through what people find there.

Small Nostr/Bitcoin projects and people who enjoy discovering them are the
initial audience hypothesis in the promotion plan. This is a starting point for
learning, not a validated customer segment or a restriction on other topics.

## What paying means

Sats express a deliberate request for attention: "I'd like you to see this."
Visibility payments influence ranking, and their ranking weight fades with time.
Optional support for the original author is separate from paid visibility.

Payment does not establish content quality, trustworthiness or human authorship.
It does not guarantee readers or a particular position. Campaign highlights and
optional discovery selections are also distinct from paid ranking.

## Nostr and the contribution flow

Nostr connects public identities, source posts and conversations across clients.
It supports the idea of an open place within a larger network. General product
introductions and the landing page should explain the board, its content and
paid visibility without requiring familiarity with Nostr. Headlines such as
"a billboard for Nostr notes" make the intended audience unnecessarily narrow.
Explain the protocol where it helps someone use the product or understand a
specific benefit. Keep its technical role clear in supporting documentation.

Mechanism examples should show choosing something to share, adding it to the
board and paying for visibility, including recommending someone else's work.
Use the selected release's actual contribution flow. Broad positioning does not
establish that every text, URL or account method is already supported.

The Add to board direction brings existing posts, text, links and quotes into a
common contribution flow. Audience descriptions can include people arriving
with text or a link, as well as people who already use Nostr. Keep actual feature
availability in the [editor guide](../web/README.md#add-to-board) and the
[changelog](../CHANGELOG.md).

The open product questions are whether readers find something useful and return,
and whether people choose to fund visibility for their own or others' posts.
Treat recurring readership and demand as questions to investigate, not established
traction.

## Explaining Holoboard

A short starting point for an introduction:

> Holoboard is a public board for posts people want others to notice. You can
> browse for free and pay to give your own post or someone else's more visibility.

Adapt this to the person and the reason for sharing. These examples are starting
points, not required slogans or tested conversion copy.

For a friend, explain what drew you to the board and show one actual post they
might care about. A personal builder introduction can connect the project to the
idea of smaller open places. Use the personal voice only when speaking as its
builder.

For a reader, lead with something concrete to investigate and invite them to
open it. Reading and joining the conversation do not require buying placement.
For a potential promoter, explain that they can draw attention to their own work
or recommend someone else's. Make paid visibility clear before asking them to
promote.

For someone familiar with Nostr, explain how the board connects to their existing
posts, identity and conversations. For other audiences, introduce the board and
its value before explaining the protocol. Match technical depth to the person.

Keep claims tied to the selected release. Direct text/link submission, account
methods and discovery features need an availability check before appearing in
an invitation. A small initial audience, future community spaces and hoped-for
repeat readership are context for the idea, not evidence of adoption.
